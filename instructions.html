HoloCube STEM Kit

Build instructions.

Everything you need to assemble the HoloCube, from unboxing the parts to uploading your first image.

What's in the box

One kit builds one HoloCube.

  • 3D printed enclosure (PLA)
  • 0.96″ OLED display (B/W)
  • ESP32 C3 Super Mini
  • 1″ beam splitting cube
  • Stranded/solid wire
  • (4) M2x4 screws
  • (4) M2x6 screws
  • (4) M2 heat set screws
  • T6 screwdriver or 1.5mm hex key

Tools you'll need

Not included in the kit.

  • Soldering kit
  • USB C data cable
  • Wire cutter and stripper
Before you start: This build involves a soldering iron, which gets hot enough to burn skin and melt materials. Students should build under adult or instructor supervision, in a ventilated area, with safety glasses on for any wire cutting or stripping.

Skills you'll learn

Soldering

Joining a wire to a component pin with heat and solder, the core skill behind almost all electronics assembly.

Wire prep

Stripping insulation and splicing stranded or solid wire cleanly enough to hold a solid connection.

Assembly Steps

Prep your wires

Cut the wire lengths you'll need to connect the OLED to the ESP32. Strip about 5mm of insulation from each end with the wire stripper, and twist stranded ends so they don't fray.

Solder the display to the microcontroller

Solder each wire from the OLED's pins (power, ground, and the two data lines) to the matching pins on the ESP32 C3 Super Mini. Double check each joint is shiny and solid, not a cold, dull blob.

Seat the electronics in the enclosure

Set the ESP32 and OLED into their printed mounts. Use the M2 heat set screws to install threaded inserts if your enclosure calls for them, then secure the board with the M2x4 screws.

Mount the beam splitter cube

Place the 1″ beam splitting cube in the top of the enclosure at the angle marked on the print. It should seat snugly in its printed mount without blocking the optical path.

Close up the case

Fit the enclosure halves together and fasten with the M2x6 screws using the T6 screwdriver or 1.5mm hex key.

Connect and upload your first image

Plug in the USB C cable to power on the board. On your phone or laptop, go into your WiFi settings and connect to the HoloCube's network, then open the page that appears to upload an image or GIF. It'll split into four quadrants and reflect together into a floating image above the cube.
